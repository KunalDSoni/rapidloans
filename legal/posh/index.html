POSH Policy

Prevention of Sexual Harassment at the Workplace

This policy is framed under The Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace (Prevention, Prohibition & Redressal) Act, 2013. The Act brings the issue of sexual harassment under legislation and provides for protection against sexual harassment of women at the workplace, and for the prevention and redressal of complaints of sexual harassment and connected matters. The onus for prevention of sexual harassment rests with the Employer or District Officer.

1. Scope

This policy applies to all employees and staff, including:

  • On-rolls / part-time, trainees, temporary / outsourced associates, whether engaged directly or through agencies, and those working in a voluntary or honorary capacity;
  • Individuals on deputation, retainership / consultancy, or contractual employment, with or without the knowledge of the Principal Employer, irrespective of whether the terms of contract are explicit or implied;
  • The scope further covers any woman in relation to Rapid premises, and thus extends to visitors, job applicants, and vendor partners.

2. Definition of Sexual Harassment

Sexual harassment includes any unwelcome act or behaviour (whether directly or by implication) such as:

  • Physical contact and advances;
  • A demand or request for sexual favours;
  • Making sexually coloured remarks;
  • Showing pornography;
  • Any other unwelcome physical, verbal, or non-verbal conduct of a sexual nature.

The following circumstances also amount to sexual harassment when implied or explicit — a promise of preferential treatment in employment; a threat of detrimental treatment in employment; a threat about present or future employment status; interfering with work or creating an intimidating, offensive, or hostile work environment; and humiliating treatment likely to affect health and safety.

3. Definitions — Others

Aggrieved: In relation to a workplace, an individual of any age, whether employed or not, who alleges sexual harassment.

Respondent: The person against whom the aggrieved woman has made the complaint.

Workplace: Government offices and private sector organisations; hospitals or nursing homes; sports institutions, stadiums, etc.; any other place visited by the employee arising out of or during the course of employment, including transportation provided by the employer for undertaking such journey; and a dwelling place or a house.

4. Forms of Sexual Harassment

Sexual harassment may be Verbal, Physical, Non-Verbal, or Visual. Physical contact is not a necessary ingredient.

5. Types of Sexual Harassment

Quid Pro Quo — Direct. Soliciting sexual favours in exchange for work benefits, where submission to such conduct is made a condition of employment or forms the basis for employment decisions, and refusal leads to retaliatory action. (Company always liable.)

Hostile Work Environment — Pervasive. Work conditions or behaviour that make the work environment hostile for the woman to be in; not a condition of employment. (Affirmative defence possible.)

6. Indicative Examples

  • Sexual advances; sexually coloured remarks, jokes, letters, phone calls, e-mail, gestures, sounds;
  • Inappropriate staring; unwelcome touching and hugging; stalking; repeated pressure for dates;
  • Sexual innuendos and taunts; display, dissemination, or transmission of matter of a sexual nature such as sexist jokes, graffiti, posters, or emails;
  • Commenting on a person's body with sexual overtones; giving inappropriate gifts; asking people to wear revealing clothes;
  • Sharing of sexual tales and experiences; asking intrusive questions about one's sexual activities;
  • Molestation, sexual sneak attacks, sexual assault, and coerced sexual intercourse.

7. Internal Committee (IC)

The Company has constituted an Internal Committee to receive and address complaints of sexual harassment, in compliance with the Act. The IC comprises a Presiding Officer (a senior woman employee), internal members, and an external member drawn from an NGO or with legal expertise in matters relating to sexual harassment.

8. Grievance Redressal — Filing a Complaint

Any aggrieved employee or associate may make a written complaint of sexual harassment at the workplace to the Internal Committee within a period of three months from the date of the incident. In case of a series of incidents, within three months from the date of the last incident.

A complaint may be made to the Internal Committee (IC), the POSH Head (Presiding Officer of the POSH committee), the Local Ethics Counsellor, or the HR Head. On receipt of a written complaint, the IC carries out a prima facie check to ascertain whether the complaint is sexual harassment in nature. If yes, the formal redressal procedure is initiated; if no, it is referred to the appropriate department and the aggrieved employee is informed accordingly.

9. Inquiry — Conciliation Process

On a written request for conciliation from the aggrieved employee, the IC facilitates conciliation between the aggrieved employee and the respondent. The settlement report is recorded and communicated to the employer for implementation, copies are shared with both parties, and proof of implementation is shared with the aggrieved for sign-off. No monetary settlement and no investigation is involved in conciliation.

10. Inquiry — Investigation Process

An investigation is triggered by a request from the aggrieved, or a breach of the conciliation terms agreed by the respondent. The IC meets the aggrieved to obtain her signature on the complaint and related documents, and the Presiding Officer advises her on the redressal procedure, rights, and responsibilities. The respondent is met, given a copy of the signed complaint, asked to acknowledge it, and informed that he must respond within 10 days with his explanation. The IC investigates the case within 60 days from the date of receipt of complaint and submits the inquiry findings report within 10 days of completing the inquiry.

11. Inquiry Outcomes

  • SH allegations proved: The IC recommends disciplinary action to the employer for implementation; copies of findings are shared with both parties; documentation and closure follow.
  • SH allegation not proved / SH did not occur: The findings report is shared with the employer; copies of findings are shared with both parties; the employer documents and closes the matter.
  • False / malicious complaint or false testimony / evidence: The IC makes recommendations to the employer, shares copies of findings with both parties, action is taken by the employer, and the matter is documented and closed.

12. Disciplinary Action

If the findings of the investigation are conclusive, the Internal Committee recommends disciplinary action to the Employer in accordance with the service rules of Rapid, ranging from a written warning letter up to and including termination of employment.

Disciplinary action will also be taken for: raising false complaints; false testimony by any party during the investigation; coaching witnesses in advance; non-cooperation in the investigation; retaliating against individuals for raising complaints or participating in the proceedings; and breach of confidentiality.

13. Employee's Roles & Responsibilities

  • Be aware of the Company's policy against sexual harassment at the workplace;
  • Do not tolerate sexual harassment — stop it and report it immediately;
  • If in doubt, talk to any member of the IC;
  • Maintain confidentiality and do not discuss the incident with colleagues or others;
  • Co-operate in the inquiry proceedings;
  • Maintain evidence of any kind — SMS, emails, photographs, etc.

If you become aware of, or witness, sexual harassment that a colleague is experiencing — report it.

14. Manager's Roles & Responsibilities

  • Be aware of the policy and spread awareness of it; encourage and empower employees to report incidents;
  • Stop inappropriate behaviour such as touching or inappropriate jokes / comments before it becomes a problem;
  • Ask team members to immediately remove any inappropriate or potentially offensive material on display — screen savers, calendars, curios, posters, etc.;
  • Be sensitive to personnel matters such as frequent attrition in a particular department, project, or team;
  • Ensure team members travelling onsite are aware of the policy;
  • Ensure proper and rigorous documentation of performance appraisals.

Report it — and immediately — even if one of the parties is a client or vendor, if a star performer is the accused, if the aggrieved is a poor performer, or if a team member reports but asks you to keep quiet or says they reported only at a personal level and not officially.

15. Our Commitment

Let us lead by example and create a safe, secure, and harassment-free working environment.
